How to Look Up US Vehicle Recalls and Complaints by VIN or Make, Model and Year

Check a car's open recalls by VIN, list every recall and owner complaint for a make, model and year, and pull the same data in bulk from the free public API.

In the US, safety recalls and owner complaints are filed with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), and the data is public and free. There are two different questions you can ask of it, and it helps to know which one you mean:

  • Is this one car affected, and has it been fixed? That needs a VIN search against the manufacturer's repair records.
  • What recalls and complaints exist for this kind of car? That is a lookup by make, model and model year, and it covers every vehicle of that line.

Check one car's open recalls (free)

  1. Find the 17 character VIN. It is on the dashboard at the driver's side corner of the windshield, on the driver's door jamb, and on the registration and insurance papers.
  2. Enter it on NHTSA's recall page at nhtsa.gov/recalls, or on the manufacturer's own recall lookup.
  3. The result shows open recalls that have not been repaired on that vehicle, or says there are none.

This is the only way to learn whether a specific car still needs a repair. If it does, contact a dealer of that brand; the recall notice says what the fix is.

List all recalls and complaints for a model (free)

NHTSA's site also lets you search by year, make and model instead of VIN. That shows the recall campaigns for the whole vehicle line, and the complaints owners have filed about it. This is useful before buying a used car, or when you want to know whether a problem you have is common.

Use the public API (free, for developers)

The same data is available as JSON with no key:

https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/recallsByVehicle?make=honda&model=accord&modelYear=2018
https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=honda&model=accord&modelYear=2018
https://vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov/api/vehicles/DecodeVinValues/YOURVIN?format=json

The third one decodes a VIN into make, model and year. Things that trip people up:

  • Model names must match exactly. The recall endpoints want the agency's spelling, such as CR-V, not crv. Some models are split, such as a decoded Silverado 1500 matching both SILVERADO 1500 and SILVERADO LD 1500.
  • Dates differ. Recall and complaint dates come in different formats.
  • There is no recall by VIN endpoint. The API answers by make, model and year. A VIN only helps by decoding to those three.

FAQ

Does a recall on my model mean my car is affected?

Not necessarily. Many recalls cover only some vehicles in a model year, for example a production date range. Use the VIN search on nhtsa.gov or the manufacturer's site to check your car.

Are owner complaints verified?

No. They are reports filed by the public. They are useful for spotting patterns, not as proof of a defect.

Why does the complaint VIN only show 11 characters?

The public data publishes a partial VIN for complaints. The full VIN is not released.

Is the recall repair free?

Safety recall repairs are normally free at the manufacturer's dealers. The recall's remedy text says what the fix is and whether it is free, so read it for your campaign and ask the dealer if anything is unclear.
