How to Analyse 16-bit Era Game Releases With pandas (Releases per Year, Genre, Region)

Build a table of one console's games from Wikipedia and Wikidata, or load a ready CSV, then answer questions like releases per year, Japan-only titles and the most common genres with a few lines of pandas.

The release history of a console is a good small dataset: a couple of thousand rows, a handful of dates and names per row, and real questions to ask. How many games came out each year? What share never left Japan? Which genres dominated, and did that change over the decade? Here is how to get the data for the Super Nintendo Entertainment System and Super Famicom era (1990 to 2000) yourself, and how to load a cleaned file and get to the questions straight away.

Step 1: Where the data is (free)

  • The English Wikipedia article "List of Super Nintendo Entertainment System games" is a big table with title, developer, and a publisher and release date per region. It is under CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • Wikidata has an item per game with genre, game mode and series, under CC0, reachable through a SPARQL endpoint.

Both are public. Scraping the table is the easy part; the hard part is what comes after.

Step 2: Scrape the Wikipedia table

import pandas as pd
url = "https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Super_Nintendo_Entertainment_System_games"
tables = pd.read_html(url)
games = max(tables, key=len)
print(games.shape, games.columns.tolist())

read_html needs lxml installed. You get a frame with multi-level columns and dates as strings like June 20, 1994, 1994-06 or just 1994, with footnotes glued on.

Step 3: Clean it

This is where the hours go:

  1. Flatten the column names and rename them to title, developers, publisher_jp, release_date_jp and so on.
  2. Strip footnote markers and bracketed notes from every text cell.
  3. Normalise dates while keeping their precision. Do not coerce 1994 to 1994-01-01; store it as the string 1994 and keep a separate first_release_year integer for grouping, or you will invent release days that never existed.
  4. Split multi-value cells (Capcom / Nintendo) into lists.
  5. Work out first_release_date and first_release_region as the earliest of the three regional dates, remembering that a year-only date sorts before a full date in the same year.
  6. Make a stable key per game, slugged from the title with a number added for duplicates.

Step 4: Join Wikidata for genres

Query the SPARQL endpoint for every item whose platform is the console, with its English label, English Wikipedia article title, genres, game modes and series. Match rows to items by the Wikipedia article the list links to first, then by exact label match where exactly one item fits. Expect about a third of rows to have no match, mostly Japan-only games. Store the Wikidata ID so you can join more later.

Step 5: The questions

With a clean frame, each question is a line or two:

df = pd.read_csv("game-releases-16bit.csv")   # | separated lists

# Releases per year
df.groupby("first_release_year").size()

# Japan-only titles
japan_only = df[df["regions"] == "JP"]
len(japan_only), len(japan_only) / len(df)

# Most common genres (explode the list field)
g = df["genres"].dropna().str.split(" \\| ").explode()
g.value_counts().head(10)

# Genre share by year
gy = df.dropna(subset=["genres"]).assign(genre=lambda d: d["genres"].str.split(" \\| ")).explode("genre")
pd.crosstab(gy["first_release_year"], gy["genre"], normalize="index").round(2)

# Publishers with the most North American releases
df["publisher_na"].dropna().value_counts().head(10)

Plot groupby("first_release_year").size() as a bar chart and the shape of the decade is obvious at a glance. Add regions as the colour and you can see when releases outside Japan stopped.

FAQ

Why not store dates as real dates?

Because the source often only knows the month or the year. Keep the string with its precision, and derive a year column for grouping. For plotting by month, parse only the rows with at least month precision.

Can I join this to sales or review data?

Yes, through wikidata_id for anything that carries Wikidata IDs, or through title with care (regional titles differ; alt_titles helps).

Why do 570 rows have no genre?

They have no matching Wikidata item, usually because they were only released in Japan and have no English Wikipedia article. Treat those genre cells as unknown, not "no genre".
