How to Keep an Indoor Grow Log for Herbs, Vegetables and Houseplants

What to record each day for plants grown indoors, how to log watering and feeding so the numbers add up, and how to see waterings per week and average temperature in a spreadsheet.

A grow log is a record of what you did and what you measured for each plant: when it started, when you watered, what you fed it, and the conditions in the room. The point is not the single entry but the pattern you can read back a month later, such as "the basil wilted every time I went five days between waterings" or "humidity dropped the week the heating came on".

This is a record-keeping guide, not growing, legal or medical advice. Keep a log only for plants it is legal for you to grow where you live.

You can do it in a notebook, a notes app or a spreadsheet. The spreadsheet version is the one that can count for you, so that is what the steps below build.

Step 1: One row per plant

A Plants table with: a unique name (Basil 1, Basil 2), start date, where it sits, the growing medium (soil, coco coir, hydroponic), its current stage (seedling, vegetative, flowering, fruiting, harvested) and an end date you fill in when it is finished.

From that, one formula gives days since start:

= IF(end_date="", TODAY() - start_date, end_date - start_date)

The end date stops the counter, so a harvested plant keeps its final age instead of counting forever.

Step 2: One row per check in a Daily log

Each time you look at a plant, one row: date, plant (a dropdown from the Plants table, so names match), watered yes or no, water amount, feed notes, pH, temperature, humidity, light hours, notes. Leave a cell empty when you did not measure it. An empty cell is left out of an average; a zero is not, and it will drag the average down.

Two habits that keep the log honest:

  • Pick one unit per column and never change it. Litres for water, one temperature scale all the way down. A spreadsheet does not know that 500 and 0.5 are the same amount.
  • Log the day you skipped watering too, with watered set to No. A gap in the log and a dry day look the same otherwise.

Step 3: A separate Feed log

Feeding is less frequent than watering and has more detail (product, amount, unit), so give it its own table: date, plant, product or mix in your own words, amount, unit, notes. Keeping it separate also means the Daily log stays quick to fill in.

Step 4: Formulas that answer the questions you actually ask

On the Plants table, per plant:

Times watered        = COUNTIFS(Daily!Plant, A2, Daily!Watered, "Yes")
Watered last 7 days  = COUNTIFS(Daily!Plant, A2, Daily!Watered, "Yes", Daily!Date, ">=" & TODAY()-6)
Waterings per week   = ROUND(times_watered / days_since_start * 7, 1)
Feed entries         = COUNTIFS(Feed!Plant, A2)

On a Summary tab: total plants, how many are harvested, waterings logged, water amount total, average temperature and humidity over every row that has a reading (AVERAGEIFS with a "<>" condition skips the blanks), and a table of the last 12 weeks with waterings and average readings per week. A bar chart of waterings per week is the quickest way to spot a week you forgot.

Step 5: Read it back

Once a week, look at the per plant numbers and the weekly table. You are looking for changes, not targets: waterings per week going up or down, a humidity average that moved, a plant whose feed entries stopped. The log does not tell you what the right numbers are; it tells you what you did, so you can connect it to how the plant looked.

FAQ

How often should I log?

Whenever you check the plants. A row with only the date, plant and "watered: No" is still useful. Readings every day are better than readings once a week, but a thin log you keep beats a full one you abandon.

Should the sheet tell me when to water?

This one does not, and a plain spreadsheet cannot know your plant, pot size or room. It shows how often you did water; whether that was right is something you judge from the plant.

Why are the formulas blank when I open the file in Excel?

Click "Enable Editing" on the yellow Protected View bar. The file is saved without cached values and Excel calculates them once editing is on.

Can I log outdoor plants with it?

Yes. The columns are the same; light hours and room temperature just mean less outside.
