How to Loop Background Music in Godot, Unity, GameMaker or RPG Maker Without a Click

Why music loops click or go silent at the join, how to cut a clean loop yourself in Audacity, which format to use, and how to set a track to repeat in each engine.

Background music in a game plays for minutes at a time, so it has to repeat. A good loop repeats without the player noticing; a bad one has a click, a gap, or a drop in level at the join every 60 seconds. The join is almost always the problem, and it comes from the file, not the engine. Here is how to make a clean loop from any track, which format to use, and the one setting per engine that makes it repeat.

Step 1: Understand what breaks a loop

  • A fade-in or fade-out. A track that starts quiet and ends quiet has a dip of silence at the join. Loops need to start and end at full level, inside the music.
  • A cut between beats. If the file ends a fraction of a beat early or late, the rhythm stumbles on every repeat.
  • A non-zero sample at the cut. When the last sample and the first sample are at different levels, the speaker jumps and you hear a click.
  • Encoder padding. MP3 encoders add a few milliseconds of silence at the start and end of every file. An MP3 cannot loop gaplessly, whatever the engine does.

Step 2: Cut a loop in Audacity (free)

  1. Open the track. Click the project rate in the bottom left and leave it at 44100 Hz.
  2. Find the tempo. Analyze, Beat Finder gives rough beat marks; a known BPM is better. One bar at 120 BPM is 2 seconds, so a 4-bar phrase is 8 seconds.
  3. Pick a start point on a downbeat after the intro, and an end point exactly a whole number of 4-bar phrases later. Zoom in and nudge both to a zero crossing (Edit, Preferences, Tracks, tick "snap to zero crossings" in older versions, or use the Z key).
  4. Select from the end point to the end of the track and delete; select from the start to the start point and delete.
  5. Crossfade the join: copy the last 100 to 150 ms of audio that came just before your start point, paste it over the final 100 to 150 ms of the loop, and apply a crossfade (Effect, Crossfade Tracks on two tracks, or Fade Out on one and Fade In on the other and mix). The end of the file now flows into the sound the start continues from.
  6. Test: select all, Transport, Looping on (shift+space), and listen through the join five or six times.
  7. Export as WAV (16-bit PCM) and as OGG Vorbis. Not MP3.

Step 3: Match loudness across tracks

A battle track and a shop track at very different levels make the player reach for the volume. Normalise each loop to about the same integrated loudness (Audacity's Loudness Normalization effect, around -14 LUFS is common for game music) while keeping the peak under -1 dBFS. Then fine-tune per scene in the engine, where quiet rooms can sit a few dB lower.

Step 4: Turn on looping in your engine

  • Godot 4: select the imported .ogg or .wav in the FileSystem dock, open the Import tab, tick Loop, click Reimport. Then play it from an AudioStreamPlayer. Leave loop offset at 0.
  • Unity: add an AudioSource, assign the clip, tick Loop and Play On Awake. For the clip, set Load Type to Streaming for long music and keep Compression Format as Vorbis.
  • GameMaker: import the OGG as a sound asset, then audio_play_sound(snd_town, 1, true); the true is loop.
  • RPG Maker MV and MZ: copy the .ogg into audio/bgm/ and choose it as the map's BGM; BGM always loops. Loop tags (LOOPSTART, LOOPLENGTH) are only needed if you want an intro that plays once, which a whole-file loop does not.

If the join is clean in the file, a whole-clip loop is all any engine needs. Loop points inside the file are for tracks with a separate intro.

Step 5: Avoid the loop fatigue

Even a perfect loop gets noticed after ten repeats. Keep loops at least 45 seconds, switch tracks between areas, lower the volume on long menu screens, and add a short silence or a stinger when a scene changes so the music feels like it moved.

FAQ

WAV or OGG in the game?

OGG for shipping: a tenth of the size and gapless. Keep the WAV as the master in case you need to edit or re-encode.

My loop still clicks in Unity.

Check the clip's Load Type. "Compressed In Memory" and "Streaming" both loop cleanly; a clip accidentally re-encoded to MP3 in the import settings does not.

Can I loop with an intro that plays once?

Yes, with two files: play the intro with loop off, and on its finished signal start the loop with loop on. Godot's finished signal, Unity's PlayScheduled with the intro's length, and RPG Maker's LOOPSTART tag all do this.

Does the music pack work in Unreal or Construct?

Any engine that plays OGG or WAV and has a loop flag works the same way.
